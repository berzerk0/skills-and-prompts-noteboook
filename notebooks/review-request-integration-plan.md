# Review request — integration plan

Paste-ready. Everything below the line goes to a fresh session that has no
history with this project. That lack of history is the point of the exercise,
so do not brief the reviewer beforehand, and do not tell them what you suspect
is wrong with the document — a reviewer told what to look for will find it
whether or not it is there.

Send it to more than one session if you can. Agreement across reviewers who
cannot see each other is worth something; a single reviewer's opinion is not.

---

You are reviewing a planning document written by someone else. You have no
context on this project, and I am not going to give you any. **That is
deliberate** — I need to know what the document communicates on its own.

The document is a plan for merging two code repositories into one. It is
[PASTE THE FILE, OR GIVE THE PATH: `notebooks/integration-plan-2026-08-25.md`].

**Do not act on it.** Do not merge anything, edit any file, run any command
against the repositories, or commit. Read and report only.

## What I want from you

Answer these five. Be specific — quote the line or name the section. "The
document is unclear in places" is useless; "line 29 says X and I cannot tell
what X refers to" is what I need.

**1. What can't you decode?**
List every term, name, or reference you could not understand from this document
alone — anything where you had to guess, or would have had to open another file
to follow the sentence.

**2. Where does it state a conclusion without the fact behind it?**
Quote any sentence that asserts something is true, decided, or risky where the
document does not give you the basis for it.

**3. If you were handed this and told to start, where would you get stuck
first?**
Walk to the first point where you would not know what to do next, and say what
information was missing.

**4. Where is opinion presented as fact or as a settled decision?**
The document contains both decisions and reasoning. Flag anywhere the two are
blurred — including any decision whose stated reason does not actually support
it. You cannot judge whether a decision is *correct* (you lack the context) and
I am not asking you to. You can judge whether a reason holds up on its own
terms.

**5. What would you do that the document does not want you to do?**
The document tries to distinguish itself from neighbouring files that are
records rather than instructions, and it tries to scope what a reader should
act on. Did that land? If you had been left alone with it, is there anything
you would have started doing that you now think was not intended?

## Do not tell me any of the following

These are the standard responses to a document like this and none of them are
useful here:

- Add a timeline, milestones, owners, or a RACI chart
- Add success metrics or KPIs
- Add a risk register or a rollback plan
- Break the phases into smaller tasks
- Suggestions about stakeholder communication
- A summary of what the document says
- General assessment of whether the plan is good

If your honest answer to a question is "nothing," say "nothing." A short
answer that is true beats a long answer that is padded.

## Format

Five numbered sections matching the five questions. Under each, a list of
specific items with a quote or line reference. No preamble, no conclusion.
